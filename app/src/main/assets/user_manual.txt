1. INTRODUCTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Logbook & Flight Tools app is designed for pilots who need to:

  • Record flight legs with all relevant details (airports, times,
    crew, aircraft, etc.).
  • Automatically calculate block time, flight time, night time and
    day time based on sunrise and sunset.
  • Automatically set takeoff and landing counts when you act as
    pilot flying.
  • Compute sunrise and sunset events along a flight route, taking
    into account the aircraft's changing position.
  • Manage a local airport database with IATA and ICAO codes, names,
    coordinates, elevation and timezone.
  • Customise how each leg card looks in the logbook list, using the
    Leg Items feature (section 11.5).
  • Record your flight experience accumulated before you started
    using the app, using the Opening Balance feature (section 14).
  • Back up and restore your logbook, and export or import data in
    CSV format for migration from other logbooks.

The app is built for professional and private pilots alike. It is
intuitive, fast, and respects your data privacy.

2. GETTING STARTED
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

After installing the app, you will see the Home screen. From here you
can access all main features:

  • Logbook – view and manage your legs.
  • Flight Sun Calculator – compute sunrise and sunset along routes.
  • Airports – manage the airport database.
  • Backup – back up and restore your data.
  • Export/Import – CSV export and import.
  • Preferences – adjust settings.
  • Updates – check for app updates.
  • User Manual – this documentation.


The first time you open an airport-related screen or use the Leg Editor,
the built-in airport database is copied automatically. You do not need
to do anything.

To add your first leg, go to the Logbook screen and tap the plus
button.

If you have flown before using this app, you can record your prior
experience in the Opening Balance screen, reached from Settings
(section 14). Once recorded, that prior experience is added to the
totals shown in the Leg List summary card, in Statistics, and in the
PDF logbook, without ever appearing as a flight leg.

Several features save files: JSON backups, CSV exports, PDF exports,
and settings backups. All of them write to a single folder on your device,
the Work Directory. You choose the Work Directory once, and the app
remembers it. See section 16.

The Home screen provides quick access to all major features through a

  • View Logbook – opens the Leg List.
  • Add New Leg – opens a blank Leg Editor.
  • Flight Sun Events – opens the calculator.
  • Settings – opens the Settings screen (Backup, Export/Import,

Every other screen has a Home button in the top-right corner of the

Up arrow on the left of the top bar returns you to the previous

When the automatic update check (section 12.2) finds a new version, a
dialog appears on the Home screen asking whether to download it. If
you tap Download, you are taken to the Updates screen where the

4. LOGBOOK LEGS – ADDING & EDITING
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Leg Editor is where you enter all the details of a flight leg.

4.1. Opening the Editor
───────────────────────

  • To add a new leg: tap the "Add New Leg" button on the Home screen.
  • To edit an existing leg: tap on any leg in the Leg List.

4.2. Fields and What They Mean
───────────────────────────────

The editor contains many fields. Below is an explanation of each:

  • Date – the date of the flight (UTC). Tap to change.

  • Flight Number – the flight number (for example, "LH123").

  • Position – your role on this leg. Suggested values are PIC, SIC,
    AugCrew, Dual and Instructor, but you can type any other value.
    When the field is empty and you tap it, the list of suggested
    values appears. As soon as you type one character, the list is
    filtered to entries containing that character. From two characters
    on, the app switches to its database suggestions and shows values
    you have used before on other legs. You can always type a value
    that is not in either list.

  • Category – the operating category of the leg. Suggested values are
    "Multi Pilot", "Single Pilot SE", "Single Pilot ME", "Helicopter"
    and "Glider", but you can type any other value. The suggestion
    behaviour is exactly the same as for Position.

  • PIC Name – Pilot in Command name.
  • SIC Name – Second in Command name.
  • Cabin Crew – names of cabin crew (optional).
  • Jumpseat 1 and 2 – names of jumpseat occupants (optional).

  • Registration – aircraft registration (for example, "D-ABCD").
    When you leave the field, the app looks up the most recently used
    Type for that registration and fills in the Type field
    automatically. You can overwrite the Type afterwards.

  • Type – aircraft type (for example, "A320"). When you leave the
    field, the app looks up the most recently used Category for that
    Type and fills in the Category field automatically. You can
    overwrite the Category afterwards.

  • Departure – departure airport (IATA or ICAO code).
  • Destination – arrival airport (IATA or ICAO code).

  • Off-Block – time when you left the parking position (UTC).
  • Takeoff – time of takeoff (UTC).
  • Landing – time of landing (UTC).
  • On-Block – time when you arrived at the parking position (UTC).
  • Sunrise and Sunset – times of sunrise and sunset (UTC). These can
    be entered manually or computed automatically using the Night
    button (see section 4.5).

  • Block Total – total block time, from Off-Block to On-Block.
  • Block Night – block time flown during night.
  • Block Day – block time flown during day, calculated automatically.

  • Flight Total – total flight time, from Takeoff to Landing.
  • Flight Night – flight time during night.
  • Flight Day – flight time flown during day, calculated
    automatically.

  • Simulator Time – time spent in simulator (if Simulator is
    checked).
  • IFR Total – total instrument flight time.
  • IFR Night – instrument flight time during night.
  • IFR Day – instrument flight time during day, calculated
    automatically as IFR Total minus IFR Night.

  • Instrument Approaches – the number of instrument approaches you
    flew on this leg. This value is required by the official logbook
    formats and appears as its own column in the PDF export. It is
    filled automatically under two conditions (see section 4.3); you
    can also overwrite it by hand at any time.

  • Takeoff counts – Day, Night, Total.
  • Landing counts – Day, Night, Total.

  • Simulator checkbox – check if this is a simulator session.
  • Pilot Flying checkbox – check if you were the pilot flying.
  • IFR checkbox – check if the flight was IFR.

  • Comment – any additional notes. If you also fill Jumpseat 1, the
    PDF logbook will print the jumpseat occupant on a second line
    labelled "J/S: ...".

4.3. Auto-Calculations
──────────────────────

The app automatically computes many fields to save you time.

  • Block Total – calculated from Off-Block and On-Block times.
  • Flight Total – calculated from Takeoff and Landing times.
  • Block Night and Flight Night – calculated using the most accurate
    method available: if you have used the Night button to compute
    sunrise and sunset, the app performs a sweep over all actual sun
    events along the route to precisely determine night intervals. If
    you manually enter sunrise and sunset times, the app falls back
    to the older static day-by-day calculation.
  • Block Day and Flight Day – derived as Total minus Night.
  • Takeoff and Landing counts – when you check Pilot Flying, the app
    sets the totals to 1 and determines whether they were day or night
    based on the takeoff and landing times.
  • IFR times – when you check IFR, the IFR Day, Night and Total
    fields are copied from the corresponding flight times. Every time
    you manually edit Flight Total or Flight Night while IFR is
    checked, the IFR values are re-copied from the flight values.
  • IFR Day – when you manually edit IFR Total or IFR Night, IFR Day
    is recomputed as IFR Total minus IFR Night.
  • Instrument Approaches – filled automatically as 1 when IFR Total
    is greater than zero and Pilot Flying is checked, but only if the
    field is currently empty. It is cleared when either condition is
    not met.
  • Simulator Time – when you check Simulator, the app calculates the
    simulator time from Off-Block and On-Block, clears the Block,
    Flight and IFR fields, and clears Instrument Approaches. If you
    have set a default simulator IFR approach in Preferences, that
    value is written into Instrument Approaches instead.

4.4. Input Formats
─────────────────

  • Time fields accept "HH:MM" (for example, "14:30") or "HHMM" (for
    example, "1430"). The app normalises your input to "HH:MM".
  • Duration fields accept "H:MM" (for example, "2:30") or "HHMM" (for
    example, "230" for 2 hours and 30 minutes). The app formats
    it as "H:MM".
  • If you enter an invalid value, the field turns yellow. Correct it
    before saving.
  • Pressing Enter on the keyboard while a field is focused clears
    the focus. This applies to every editable field in the Leg Editor.
    If the field has an automatic lookup attached, the lookup runs at
    that moment.

The same time and duration rules apply inside the Search value dialog.

4.5. Using the Night Button
───────────────────────────

The Night button next to Sunrise and Sunset launches the Flight Sun
Calculator with the current departure airport, destination airport,
takeoff time and landing time already filled in. It also sends the
Off-Block and On-Block times so the calculator can use them for
ground-interval night computation.

After computing the sunrise and sunset events, you can tap Apply to
Leg. This transfers the moving sunrise and sunset events along the
flight route, the static sunrise and sunset at the departure and
arrival airports, and the day or night status at Off-Block, Takeoff,
Landing and On-Block.

The leg editor then uses this data to recalculate Block Night and
Flight Night using the sweep-based method, and to automatically set
the takeoff and landing day or night counts if Pilot Flying is
checked. The sunrise and sunset fields in the leg are also updated.

If you manually enter sunrise and sunset times instead of using the
tool, the app falls back to the older static calculation.

4.6. Saving a Leg
────────────────

  • Tap Save to store the leg in your logbook.
  • If any field is invalid, you will see a warning. Fix the marked
    fields and try again.

4.7. View Mode vs Edit Mode
───────────────────────────

  • When you open an existing leg, it is in view mode. You can see
    all fields but cannot change them.
  • Tap Edit to switch to edit mode, make changes, then tap Save.
  • Tap Cancel to discard changes and return to view mode.
  • For a new leg, you are directly in edit mode.

4.8. Deleting a Leg
──────────────────

  • In edit mode, you can tap Delete to remove the leg. A
    confirmation dialog appears to prevent accidental deletion.

4.9. Adding a New Leg: Default Values
─────────────────────────────────────

When you open a new, empty leg, some fields are pre-filled with values
you configured in Preferences (sections 11.1 and 11.2):

  • Position – filled with the default position, if one is configured.
  • IFR checkbox – set according to the "Default is IFR" preference.

For an existing leg that you open for editing, your stored values are
kept. The default position is only applied to new legs.

5. LEG LIST – VIEWING, FILTERING & STATISTICS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Leg List is the main logbook screen. It shows all your recorded
legs, sorted by date.

5.1. Summary Information
────────────────────────

At the top of the list, you will see:

  • Total Block Time (sum of all block times).
  • Total Block Night Time.
  • Total Simulator Time.
  • Number of legs.

These summaries update automatically based on the current filter.

When the filter is All, the Opening Balance (section 14) is added to
the Total Block Time, Total Block Night Time and Total Simulator Time.
The number of legs is never affected by the Opening Balance.

When the filter is anything other than All, the Opening Balance is not
included.

5.2. Filtering
──────────────

You can filter the list to show only legs from a specific period:

  • All – shows every leg.
  • Last 7 days, Last 28 days – shows legs from the last week or
    month.
  • Actual Month – shows legs from the current calendar month.
  • Previous Month – shows legs from the previous calendar month.
  • Actual Year – shows legs from the current year.
  • Custom – lets you pick a start and end date.

Tap the filter button next to the search icon to change the filter.

5.3. Searching
──────────────

Tap the search button to open the advanced search. You build a list of
conditions, one at a time, and combine them with AND or OR logic.

Building a condition:

  1. Tap an operator button: equals or contains, is not or does not
     contain, is on or before, is on or after.
  2. Pick a field from the list. For the ordering operators, only
     time, duration and number fields are offered.
  3. Enter a value.

The value entry differs by field type:

  • Text fields: type the value. For equals and not-equals,
    suggestions from your own logbook appear after two characters.
    You can always type a value that is not in the suggestions.
  • Boolean fields: choose true or false from the dropdown.
  • Time fields: a date field and a time field. Tap the date to open
    a date picker; type the time in "HH:MM" or "HHMM". A Clear button
    empties both. You can fill only the date, only the time, or both.
  • Duration fields: type the value in "H:MM" or "HHMM".

Tap OK to add the condition. It appears in the Conditions list with a
delete button.

The switch at the bottom selects AND or OR. AND requires every
condition to be true; OR requires at least one.

Tap Search to open the results. The result screen has a "Search
Results" title and hides the date filter chips. Tap Back to return to
the search screen and adjust your conditions.

Known limitation: a not-equals condition does not include legs whose
field is empty. To include them, add a second condition for the same
field with equals and an empty value, and use OR logic.

Search never includes the Opening Balance. Only actual legs are
filtered.

5.4. Statistics
───────────────

Tap the statistics button to view aggregated statistics. Two modes:

  • By Type – one row per statistic (Block, Block Night, Flight Time,
    IFR, VFR, counters, and so on), one column per Position, plus a
    Total column. Which rows are shown depends on your Statistics
    Visibility preferences (section 11.3).

    If you have recorded an Opening Balance (section 14), the balance
    contributes to the By Type view as follows. Rows of the Opening
    Balance that have a position contribute to the corresponding
    position column. Rows that have no position appear under a
    dedicated column named Prior, which is separate from the Unknown
    column that belongs to legs. The Opening Balance is always
    included in the By Type view, regardless of the Leg List filter.

  • By Month/Year – one row per year with Block Total, Block Night,
    number of legs, and Simulator time. Tap a year to expand it into
    one row per month.

    If you have recorded an Opening Balance, a single Prior row is
    added to this list. It shows the totals of every Opening Balance
    row. It does not expand; it has no months. Its placement depends
    on the earliest start date you entered for the balance: it
    appears at the position where its year would fall relative to the
    year headers. If you did not enter a start date for any balance
    row, the Prior row is placed at the bottom of the list.

The Legs count excludes simulator sessions. Simulator time still
appears in its own column.

The Month/Year view always shows your whole logbook; it does not use
the date filter from the Leg List. It always includes the Opening
Balance.

5.5. Customising the Leg Cards
──────────────────────────────

Each leg in the list is displayed as a small card with four lines of
information. You can choose which fields appear on each line, in which
order, and with which separators, using the Leg Items feature. See
section 11.5 for details. The change applies the next time you open
the Leg List.

6. FLIGHT SUN CALCULATOR
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Flight Sun Calculator computes sunrise, sunset, astronomical
dawn and astronomical dusk along your flight route, taking into
account the aircraft's changing position and cruise altitude. This
helps you determine accurate day and night times for the whole
flight, not just for the departure and arrival points.

6.1. When to Use It
───────────────────

Use this tool when you need accurate sun times for a specific flight,
especially when the flight crosses time zones, spans a long distance,
or crosses into or out of night.

6.2. How to Open the Calculator
───────────────────────────────

  • From the Leg Editor, tap the Night button. The calculator is
    pre-filled with the leg's departure airport, destination, take-off
    time and landing time, and shows the Off-Block and On-Block times
    for reference.
  • From the Home screen, tap Flight Sun Events.

6.3. Input Fields
─────────────────

  • Departure and Destination – enter the airport codes. The app
    autocompletes from the airport database.
  • T/O Date and T/O Time – the date and time of take-off (UTC).
  • Ldg Date and Ldg Time – the date and time of landing (UTC). The
    app ensures landing is after take-off and adjusts dates if needed.
  • Altitude – the cruising altitude in feet. This affects the
    horizon used for sunrise and sunset, so a higher altitude results
    in slightly earlier sunrises and later sunsets.
  • Off-Block and On-Block (display only) – shown when the calculator
    was opened from the Leg Editor.
  • Waypoints – optional intermediate points. See section 6.4.

6.4. Waypoints
──────────────

Waypoints improve accuracy by telling the calculator where the
aircraft is at specific moments. Tap Add Waypoint(s) to add a row.
Each row has three fields:

  • Latitude – for example N45°02.4, S32°06.0, -4512, or 45.04.
  • Longitude – for example E012°45.3, W075°30.0, -012453, or 12.755.
  • UTC – the time at that point in "HH:MM" format.

You can fill any combination:

  • Both coordinates and time – the row is a known point at a known
    time.
  • Both coordinates, no time – the time is filled in by the
    calculator from the surrounding times.
  • Time only, no coordinates – the position is filled in by the
    calculator along a great-circle arc between the surrounding known
    points.
  • Only one coordinate – the missing coordinate field is marked with
    a yellow warning. Correct it or clear the other one before
    computing.

Coordinate formats
  The app accepts many formats for coordinates. You can type decimal
  degrees (45.04), compact forms (4512 means 45°12'), or hemisphere
  and degree/minute formats (N45°02.4). A leading minus sign is
  treated as South or West. If you use a minus sign and a hemisphere
  letter that contradict each other, the field is marked as invalid.

Time anchoring
  Every waypoint time is anchored to the take-off date of the flight.
  If the time you enter is earlier in the day than take-off, one day
  is added automatically. If the resulting time is still before
  take-off, or is after landing, the field is marked with a yellow
  warning. A flight longer than 24 hours cannot be used with waypoint
  times and produces a warning.

Sorting
  Once every non-empty waypoint row has a time that you entered
  yourself, the rows rearrange themselves by time automatically. Rows
  that are completely empty are removed at that moment.

Removing a waypoint
  Tap the X button on the right of a row to remove it.

6.5. Computing the Results
──────────────────────────

Tap Compute Sunrise / Sunset. The app calculates the solar elevation
at take-off and landing to determine if they are by day or by night,
scans the entire route minute by minute to find any sunrise, sunset,
astronomical dawn or astronomical dusk crossings, and interpolates
the exact time and position of each event. It also displays the
static sunrise and sunset at the departure and arrival airports for
the given date.

6.6. Understanding the Results
──────────────────────────────

  • The results text shows a list of events found, each with its UTC
    time and the position where it occurred.
  • It also tells you if Off-Block, Take-off, Landing and On-Block
    are by day or by night.
  • Below the results, the static fields show Astro Dawn, Sunrise,
    Sunset and Astro Dusk at the departure and arrival airports.
  • Waypoint fields that were filled in by the calculator are shown
    in a slightly different style to indicate they are temporary.
    Tap them and leave them to confirm them as your own entries.

6.7. Applying Results to a Leg
──────────────────────────────

If you opened the calculator from the Leg Editor, you will see an
Apply to Leg button after computing. Tapping it sends back all moving
sunrise and sunset events, the static sunrise and sunset at departure
and arrival airports, and the day or night status for Off-Block,
Take-off, Landing and On-Block. The leg editor then recalculates
Block Night and Flight Night using the most accurate method and
updates the take-off and landing day or night counts.

6.8. Saving and Loading State
─────────────────────────────

You can save all inputs using the Save State button. Later, you can
restore them with Load State. The saved data includes the airports,
dates, times, altitude and all waypoints.

6.9. Clearing
─────────────

Tap Clear to reset the calculator: airports, times and waypoints are
cleared, and altitude is set to your default.

7. AIRPORT DATABASE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The app uses a local airport database that contains IATA and ICAO
codes, names, cities, countries, coordinates, elevation and timezone.
This database is used for autocomplete in the Leg Editor and the
Flight Sun Calculator.

7.1. Built-in Database
──────────────────────

A default airport database is included with the app. It is copied to
internal storage on first launch, so you have a working set of
airports immediately.

7.2. Updating the Database
──────────────────────────

You can manually update to the latest version from the web:

  1. Go to the Airports screen.
  2. Tap Update Airport DB.
  3. The app downloads the latest database.
  4. After a successful download, the date of the update is shown in
     the status text.

7.3. Managing the Database
──────────────────────────

The Airports screen offers several management options:

  • Edit Airport Database – opens the Airport Editor.
  • Export Airports – saves the current merged database as a JSON file
	to your Downloads folder. No save dialog appears; the file is
	written	directly. The filename is airports_export_<number>.json.
	A toast confirms the export.
  • Clear User Modifications – removes all airports you have added,
    edited or deleted.
  • Delete Entire Database – permanently removes the entire airport
    database.

7.4. Airport Editor
───────────────────

The Airport Editor allows you to:

  • Search – type a partial IATA, ICAO, city or airport name. Results
    appear in a dropdown.
  • Add – fill in the required fields and tap Save.
  • Edit – select an airport, tap Edit, modify fields, then Save.
    Note: ICAO cannot be changed. To change ICAO, delete and re-add.
  • Delete – select an airport, tap Delete, and confirm.

All changes are stored immediately and reflected in autocomplete.

8. BACKUP & RESTORE (JSON)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Backup and Restore works entirely with JSON files and is independent
of CSV export and import.

A backup contains your logbook legs and your Opening Balance.
Restoring an old backup (created before the Opening Balance feature
existed) restores only the legs and leaves your Opening Balance
untouched. Restoring a new backup restores both.

8.1. Creating a Backup
──────────────────────

  1. Go to the Backup screen.
  2. Select a backup folder using the system folder picker. This
     folder is remembered.
  3. Tap Backup. The app creates a JSON file containing all legs with
     a timestamp.
  4. The app verifies the backup before considering it complete. It
     reads the file back, checks that the content is valid, and
     compares the number of legs with your database.
  5. The app keeps the 10 most recent backups and deletes older ones
     automatically.

Your logbook is also backed up automatically before any operation
that could change your data:

  • Before importing a CSV file (section 9).
  • Before downloading and installing an app update (section 12).

In both cases the same verification applies. If the backup cannot be
verified, the operation is blocked.

8.2. Restoring from a Backup
────────────────────────────

  8.2. Restoring from a Backup

    1. In the Backup screen, tap Restore.
    2. Confirm that you want to replace all current data.
    3. If your Work Directory contains backup files, a list of
       them appears, newest first. Pick one.
       If it does not, a "No backups found" message appears with
       a "Browse elsewhere" button.
    4. You can also tap "Browse elsewhere" at any time to pick a
       backup file from anywhere on your device (for example a
       file you copied from another phone).
    5. The app clears the legs table and inserts all legs from
       the backup. The Opening Balance is replaced only if the
       backup file contains an Opening Balance section. Old-
       format backups restore legs only and leave the Opening
       Balance untouched.

8.3. Deleting All Data
──────────────────────

In the Backup screen, there is a Delete All button. This permanently
removes all logbook legs. A confirmation dialog prevents accidental
deletion.

The Delete All action removes legs only. Your Opening Balance is not
removed by this action. To remove the Opening Balance, use
Settings → Opening Balance (section 14).

9. CSV EXPORT / IMPORT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━


  9.1. Export CSV

    Tap Export CSV on the Export/Import screen. The app writes a
    CSV file directly to your Work Directory. No save dialog
    appears. The filename is logbook_export_<date>.csv. The file
    contains a column for Instrument Approaches. Timestamps are
    written in the standard ISO 8601 format in UTC.

    CSV export covers legs only. The Opening Balance is not
    included.

    If no Work Directory has been set yet, a folder picker
    opens; after you pick, the export continues automatically.

  9.2. Import CSV
    Tap Import CSV. A list of CSV files in your Work Directory
    appears, newest first. Pick the one to import. You can also
    tap "Browse elsewhere" to pick a CSV file from anywhere on
    your device.

    After you pick, the CSV Import screen opens with a mapping
    list:
      • A list of columns from the CSV header is shown.
      • For each column, a dropdown lets you map it to a
        corresponding field in the app. You can skip columns by
        selecting None.
      • Once all required fields are mapped, tap Import.

    CSV import creates legs only. It never creates Opening
    Balance rows.

    Automatic mapping for app-generated files
      If the CSV file was produced by this app, columns are
      recognised and mapped automatically.

    Backup before import
      When you tap Import, the app first creates a verified JSON
      backup of your current logbook in your Work Directory. The
      import only starts if the backup is confirmed to be valid.

10. PDF LOGBOOK EXPORT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The app produces a printable logbook page in the ICAO and EASA layout
prescribed by EASA AMC1 FCL.050, in landscape A4. Every aspect of the
printed page can be adjusted before exporting.

  10.1. Exporting

    1. From the Export/Import screen, tap Export PDF.
    2. Choose what to include:
         • All – every leg in the database.
         • From – To – only legs whose Off-Block date falls
           inside the chosen dates.
         • Last N pages – only the last N printed pages.
    3. Tap Generate. The app builds the PDF and writes it
       directly to your Work Directory. No save dialog appears.
       The filename is logbook_<date>.pdf. A toast confirms the
       export.

    If no Work Directory has been set yet, a folder picker
    opens first; after you pick, the range dialog appears.

10.2. What Appears on Each Page
───────────────────────────────

  • Header, left: "Owner: <name>". Hidden when the owner name is
    empty.
  • Header, centre: "Pilot Logbook".
  • Header, right: the date range of the legs printed on that page.
  • Table header with two rows.
  • One row per leg.
  • Three totals rows at the bottom of every page:
       1. Total this page – the sum of every numeric column for the
          legs printed on this page.
       2. Total from previous page – every leg in the database up to
          the end of the previous printed page, plus the Opening
          Balance.
       3. Total time – every leg in the database up to the end of the
          current page, plus the Opening Balance.
  • Merged Remarks cell across the three totals rows containing the
    certification sentence, your signature if set, and your name at
    the bottom.
  • Page number at the bottom centre of the page.

The Opening Balance (section 14) is never printed as a leg row. It
only contributes to the running totals. Because of this, the very
first printed page's Total from previous page already holds your
prior experience. For a From-To range, the Opening Balance is added
in full, regardless of whether its own start and end dates fall
inside the chosen range.

This behaviour applies to all three export ranges: All, From-To, and
Last N pages. For a From-To range, the Opening Balance is added in
full, regardless of whether its own start and end dates fall inside
the chosen range.

10.3. About Last N Pages
────────────────────────

This mode prints only the most recent N pages of your logbook, but the
pages keep the numbers and the running totals they would have in the
full logbook. For example, if your logbook currently fills 20 pages
and you export the last 3, the resulting PDF contains pages numbered
18/20, 19/20 and 20/20. Each of those pages contains exactly the rows,
the totals and the page number that it would have if you exported the
whole logbook.

The Total from previous page row on page 18 includes every leg that
was on pages 1 to 17, plus the Opening Balance, even though those
pages are not in the file.

If you enter a number larger than the current page count, the whole
logbook is printed instead.

10.4. Configuring the PDF Layout
────────────────────────────────

From the same Export/Import screen, tap Format PDF. The settings
screen lets you control:

  • Rows per page – 13, 15, 18, 20 or 25. Default 18.
  • PDF font – Sans-serif, Serif or Monospace.
  • Alternate row background – shades every other leg row light grey.
  • Owner name – printed in the header and at the bottom of the
    Remarks cell.
  • Signature – tap Set Signature and sign on the screen. Tap Set
    Signature again to replace it, or Remove to delete it.
  • Page layout – margins, title height, header row height, page
    number area.
  • Column widths – date, place, time, aircraft model, aircraft
    registration, duration, name of PIC, count, remarks.
  • Text sizes – title, header, body, remarks, certification, owner.

Beneath the column widths, the app shows a live scale indicator. The
scale factor is applied to every column width and every text size, so
the layout always fits the page edge to edge.

Set Defaults restores the layout fields (margins, heights, widths,
text sizes, and font) to the factory values, setting the font to
Sans-serif. It does not change your selected rows per page, owner
name, or signature. Tap Save to keep your changes.

The Leg Items customization (section 11.5) affects only the on-screen
Leg List; it does not change the PDF layout.

11. PREFERENCES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Access Preferences from the Settings screen.

11.1. Default Fields
────────────────────

Default Fields defines the values that a new, empty leg starts with.
It does not affect existing legs.

  • Default is IFR – when checked, the IFR checkbox is ticked for
    every new leg.
  • Default Simu IFR Approach – the value written into Instrument
    Approaches when a leg is a simulator session. The default when
    never set is 5.
  • Default Position – the value written into Position for a new leg.

Tap Save to store all three settings. They are read when you open a
new leg.

11.2. Custom Lists
──────────────────

Custom Lists lets you edit the suggested values for the Position and
Category fields in the Leg Editor. The lists are only suggestions: you
can always type any value in a leg.

The same suggestion lists are used by the Opening Balance editor
(section 14) for its Position and Category fields.

The screen has two sections, one for Position and one for Category.
Each section lists one row per value, with a remove button and an Add
button.

  • Reset to defaults – replaces the rows of the corresponding section
    with the built-in default list.
  • Save – trims each value, drops empty rows, and drops duplicates
    while preserving your order.

The built-in default lists are:

  Position:   PIC, SIC, AugCrew, Dual, Instructor
  Category:   Multi Pilot, Single Pilot SE, Single Pilot ME,
              Helicopter, Glider

Changes apply the next time you open the Leg Editor or the Opening
Balance editor; already-open editors keep the lists they started with.

11.3. Statistics Visibility
───────────────────────────

You can choose which statistics appear in the By Type statistics view.
Uncheck any stat you don't want to see – it will be hidden. This does
not affect the Month/Year view.

11.4. Sun Calculator Default Altitude
─────────────────────────────────────

Set the default altitude in feet for the Flight Sun Calculator. The
default is 37000 feet.

11.5. Leg Items
───────────────

The Leg Items screen lets you customise the four lines of information
shown on every leg card in the Leg List. It does not change the leg
data itself, and it does not affect the PDF export.

11.6. Backup / Restore Settings
───────────────────────────────
    Access: Preferences → Backup / Restore Settings.

    This screen lets you save all your settings to a single JSON
    file in your Work Directory, and restore them later. It is
    separate from the logbook backup (section 8): the logbook
    backup saves flights and Opening Balance; the settings
    backup saves preferences.

    Save Settings
      Reads every preference and writes logbook_prefs_<date>.json
      to your Work Directory. Includes default field values,
      custom lists, Leg Items layout, PDF format, statistics
      visibility, update settings, default altitude, and your
      signature image (as PNG, stored inside the JSON file).

    Restore Settings
      Shows a confirm dialog ("This will replace your current
      settings with the backup. Continue?").
      After confirming, a list of logbook_prefs_*.json files in
      your Work Directory appears, newest first. Pick one.
      You can also tap "Browse elsewhere" to pick a settings
      file from anywhere on your device.

    What gets replaced
      The settings that are present in the file overwrite your
      current values. Settings absent from the file are left
      untouched. Your Work Directory choice is not changed.

    Signature
      If the backup includes a signature, it replaces your
      current signature. If the backup contains no signature,
      your current signature is removed. If the signature cannot
      be applied (for example the file is corrupt), the rest of
      the settings are still restored and a warning appears.

Opening Leg Items
─────────────────

From the Settings screen: Preferences → Leg Items.

The screen shows:

  • A profile selector at the top.
  • Buttons to manage profiles: New, Duplicate, Rename, Delete.
  • Four row editors, one for each line of the leg card.
  • A live preview card at the bottom.
  • Restore Default and Save buttons.

Profiles
────────

A profile is a saved configuration of the four lines. You can keep
several profiles and switch between them. Only one profile is active
at a time.

  • Default – a built-in profile that matches the app's original
    layout. It cannot be edited and cannot be deleted.
  • New – creates a new profile.
  • Duplicate – same as New.
  • Rename – changes the name of the current profile.
  • Delete – removes the current profile. You cannot delete the
    profile that is currently active.

Editing a Row
─────────────

Each of the four rows shows a list of items. An item is either a field
(a value taken from the leg) or a text (a literal string you type).

  • Add Field – opens a dialog to add a field.
  • Add Text – opens a dialog to add a literal text.
  • Move up, move down, delete – reorder or remove an item.
  • Tap an item to edit it.

The Preview Card
────────────────

The preview card uses a fixed sample leg so you always see something
meaningful. It updates every time you change a row.

Saving
──────

  • Save commits the current profile and makes it the active one.
  • If you try to leave the screen with unsaved changes, the app asks
    whether to Save, Discard, or Cancel.

12. UPDATES & VERSION MANAGEMENT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Updates screen checks for new versions of the app. When a new
version is available, you can download and install it directly. The
app uses a secure HTTPS connection to fetch version information.

12.1. Checking Manually
───────────────────────

Tap Check for App Update at any time. The app contacts the update
server and shows one of the following messages: no update available,
invalid updates information, could not reach update server, or an
update-available dialog.

Every manual check records the current date and time in the Last Check
line at the bottom of the screen.

12.2. Automatic Checks
──────────────────────

The app can also check for updates on its own, in the background. Two
switches at the top of the Updates screen control this:

  • Automatic check for updates – master switch. When OFF, the app
    never checks automatically. When ON (the default), automatic
    checks are allowed.
  • Check on every app start – only meaningful when the master switch
    is ON. When ON, the app checks every time you open it. When OFF
    (the default), the app checks at most once every 7 days.

12.3. The Last Check Line
─────────────────────────

At the bottom of the Updates screen, you will see either "Last check:
never" or "Last check: dd.MM.yyyy HH:mm". The date is shown in UTC.

12.4. When an Update Is Found
─────────────────────────────

Whether the update was found by an automatic or manual check,
the flow is the same from the moment the update is confirmed:

      1. The app prepares a verified backup of your logbook in
         your Work Directory. If no Work Directory is set, a
         folder picker opens; the backup and download resume
         once you pick. If the backup cannot be verified, the
         update does not proceed.
      2. A dialog appears with the title "Update Available".
         Two buttons: Download and Later.
      3. If you tap Download, the update is handed to your
         device's download service. You can leave the app — the
         download continues in the background, and a system
         notification shows its progress.
      4. When the download finishes, the system notification
         changes to completed. Tap it to open the file. Your
         device's installer (or Files app) opens and offers to
         install the update.

    If you tap Later, nothing else happens. The next auto-check
    will respect the 7-day throttle.


12.5. About the Download
──────────────────────────

    Updates are always downloaded to your device's Downloads
    folder. The file is opened by the system when you tap the
    completed notification. You can also find it in your
    Downloads folder or Files app at any time.

    The app does not download in the background on its own.
    Downloads only start when you tap Download in the update
    dialog.

13. OPENING BALANCE (PRIOR EXPERIENCE)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The Opening Balance records flight experience you accumulated before
you started using this app. Its purpose is to make the totals shown in
the Leg List, in Statistics, and in the PDF logbook reflect your full
career, not just the flights you entered into the app.

The Opening Balance is never shown as a flight leg.

13.1. How to Reach It
  Settings → Opening Balance.

13.2. Two Ways to Enter Your Prior Experience
  Detailed entry: one row per aircraft type, position, category, or
  any grouping that makes sense to you.

  Last-total-line entry: a single row holding the cumulative totals
  printed on the last page of your paper logbook. Leave the type,
  position and category empty in that row.

13.3. Adding a Row
  From the list, tap Add. Fill in any of the fields you need. All
  fields are optional.

13.4. What Fills Itself In
  Block, Flight, IFR groups: when you enter a value and leave the
  field, the missing value is calculated from the other two (Night
  is the reference).

  Takeoff and Landing groups: same rule.

  Category and pilot-function times: when you enter a block total
  and a category, the three Single-Pilot and Multi-Pilot fields are
  filled in. When you enter a block total and a position, the four
  Pilot Function fields are filled in. Recalculations replace any
  value you typed earlier.

13.5. Editing a Row
  Tap any row to open it. Change any field, then Save. Tap Cancel to
  discard changes. Tap Delete to remove the row.

13.6. How the Opening Balance Affects the Rest of the App
  • Leg List summary card: added to the three totals when the filter
    is All.
  • Statistics By Type: contributes to the matching position column,
    or to a Prior column when the row has no position.
  • Statistics By Month/Year: a Prior row shows the balance totals.
  • PDF logbook: contributes to the running totals, never printed as
    a leg row.

13.7. Things to Keep in Mind
  • The Opening Balance is part of a JSON backup. Restoring an old
    backup (created before this feature) leaves it untouched.
  • The Opening Balance is not part of CSV export or import.
  • Delete All on the Backup screen removes legs only.

14. FILES AND DATA STORAGE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

All data is stored locally on your device. No data is sent to any
server. Your privacy is fully respected.

14.1. Internal Storage
─────────────────────

    The app stores its private data in its internal storage
    directory:
      • logbook_database – SQLite database containing legs and
        Opening Balance.
      • airports.json – base airport database (created on first
        airport-related use).
      • airports_user.json – your airport modifications.
      • airports.cache – serialized cache of the merged airport
        database.
      • pdf_signature.png – your signature image (if set).

    Your Leg Items profiles and every other preference are
    stored in the app's private preferences.

14.2. Work Directory and Export Files
─────────────────────────────────────
    A single folder, called the Work Directory, receives every
    file the app creates for you:
      • logbook_backup_*.json – logbook backups.
      • logbook_export_*.csv – CSV exports.
      • logbook_*.pdf – PDF exports.
      • logbook_prefs_*.json – settings backups.

    The Work Directory is a subfolder named PilotLogbookFoss
    inside the folder you pick. You pick the parent folder once.
    The app keeps the 10 most recent logbook backups and deletes
    older ones automatically; the other files are not rotated.

    Airport database exports (section 7.3) and update APK downloads
    go to your device's Downloads folder instead, and are not part
    of the Work Directory.

14.3. Clearing Data
───────────────────

The app provides functions to clear data. Each is separate:

  • Backup → Delete All – deletes legs only.
  • Airports → Clear User Modifications – deletes your airport
    modifications.
  • Airports → Delete Entire Database – deletes the whole airport
    database.
  • Settings → Opening Balance – delete rows one by one from the list.

Clearing the logbook does not remove your Leg Items profiles, your
Opening Balance, or your update-check settings.

14.4. First Launch
───────────────────
    On first launch, the app does not copy the airport database
    yet, does not create a Work Directory, and does not create
    any export or backup file. The airport database is copied
    the first time an airport-related screen opens. The Work
    Directory is created the first time you use a feature that
    needs it (Backup, CSV, PDF, Updates, or Backup / Restore
    Settings) and you pick a parent folder.

15.1. Common Issues
───────────────────

Logbook — input and saving
  • Invalid input fields – if a field turns yellow, the format is
    incorrect. Check section 4.4 and correct it.
  • Instrument Approaches fills with 1 or clears unexpectedly – this
    is by design. It is set to 1 when IFR Total is greater than zero
    and Pilot Flying is ticked, but only if the field is currently
    empty. It is cleared when either condition is not met.
  • IFR fields do not update when I edit Flight Total or Flight Night
    – IFR fields are only copied from flight time when the IFR
    checkbox is ticked.

Opening Balance
  • The Prior row in Statistics Month/Year is not where I expected –
    its position depends on the earliest start date you entered. If
    you did not enter a start date, the Prior row appears at the
    bottom of the list.
  • The Leg List summary card does not include my Opening Balance –
    the balance is only added when the filter is All.
  • My Opening Balance is missing after restoring a backup – this is
    by design for backups created before the feature existed. New
    backups include the Opening Balance.
  • My Opening Balance is still present after using Delete All on the
    Backup screen – Delete All removes legs only. To remove the
    Opening Balance, open Settings → Opening Balance and delete each
    row individually.
  • The first page's Total from previous page is not zero – this is
    by design. If you have recorded an Opening Balance, it appears in
    that row.

Airports
  • Airport not found – the airport may not be in the database.
    Update the database or add it manually.

Backup and CSV
  • Backup fails – check that you have granted storage permissions,
    if required, and that the backup folder is accessible.
  • CSV import blocked – if the app cannot create and verify a backup
    of your current logbook before importing, the import will not
    start.

PDF
  • PDF fonts look different from what I chose – if the font file is
    unavailable, the app falls back to the built-in Sans-serif.
    Reopen Format PDF and confirm your choice is still selected.
  • Numbers do not line up perfectly in the PDF – choose Monospace
    in Format PDF; the digits then occupy identical widths.
  • Last N pages exported fewer or more pages than expected – the
    number is the count of printed pages, not the count of flight
    legs. If you enter a number greater than the total pages your
    logbook currently fills, the whole logbook is printed.
  • The first page's total from previous page is not zero – this is
    by design. If you have recorded an Opening Balance, it appears in
    that row.

Search
  • My search for a specific time returns legs on every date – this
    is by design. A time-only timestamp condition compares the time
    of day, ignoring the date.
  • Not-equals does not include legs whose field is empty – known
    limitation. Add a second condition for the same field with equals
    and an empty value, and use OR logic.

Statistics
  • A statistic is missing from the By Type view – check that it is
    ticked on Preferences → Statistics Visibility.
  • The Legs count is lower than the number of legs I entered – the
    count excludes simulator sessions by design.
  • The Month/Year view shows the whole logbook even though I
    filtered the Leg List – by design. Only By Type uses the Leg List
    filter.
  • There is a Prior row at the top or in the middle of the year list
    – this is your Opening Balance. Its position is determined by the
    earliest start date you entered for the balance.

Leg Items
  • My Leg List still shows the old card layout after I saved a new
    profile – the Leg List picks up the active profile the next time
    it is opened.
  • The Delete button on Leg Items shows "Can NOT Delete Active
    Profile" – you cannot delete the profile currently in use. Switch
    to a different profile first.
  • I cannot edit the Default profile – by design.
  • Position or Category suggestions do not include a value I added
    in Custom Lists – changes to Custom Lists apply the next time you
    open the Leg Editor or the Opening Balance editor.

Updates:

  • The app never checks for updates automatically — open
    Updates and check that Automatic check for updates is ON.
  • The app is not checking on every start — Check on every app
    start may be OFF, or the 7-day timer has not expired yet.
  • Last check does not change when I open the app — by design.
    An automatic check that cannot reach the server does not
    count.
  • Update Available dialog reappears after I rotate my phone —
    by design. The dialog is preserved across rotation.
  • I tapped Download but nothing happened — the download
    service did not accept the request. Retry. The file is not
    on your device until the system notification shows
    progress.
  • I tapped Download and closed the app — the download
    continues in the background. Look at the system
    notification.
  • My update installed automatically — it did not. The app
    never installs silently. The system notification opens the
    file; you must confirm the install in the system installer.

16. WORK DIRECTORY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

  The Work Directory is where the app writes every logbook-
  related file: JSON backups, CSV exports, PDF exports, and
  settings backups. You choose it once, and the app remembers.

  How to set it
    Open any of the following:
      • Settings → Work Directory
      • Settings → Backup → Backup Logbook
      • Settings → Export/Import → Export CSV, Export PDF, or
        Import CSV
      • Preferences → Backup / Restore Settings → Save Settings
        or Restore Settings
      • Settings → App Update, if an update is found

    A folder picker opens at your Downloads folder. Tap "Use
    this folder" to accept, or navigate elsewhere first. The app
    creates a subfolder named PilotLogbookFoss inside the folder
    you pick. All logbook files live there.

  How to change it
    Open Settings → Work Directory. The picker opens at your
    current folder. Pick a new one. Files already written stay
    in the old folder; new files go to the new one.

  What happens if the folder becomes unavailable
    If the folder is deleted or its permission is revoked, the
    next time you use a feature that needs it the app tells you
    and asks you to pick it again. Files already written inside
    it are not deleted by the app.

  What goes to Downloads instead
    Airport database exports and, if you disable auto-install,
    update APK downloads. These are one-off files that go to
    your Downloads folder, not the Work Directory.

── NEW §17 SETTINGS BACKUP / RESTORE ──

  Settings Backup lets you save your preferences to a file and
  restore them later, for example when you move to a new phone.
  It is separate from the logbook backup.

  What it saves
    Update settings, default Leg Editor fields, custom suggest
    lists, Leg Items card layout, PDF format settings, statistics
    visibility, default Sun Calculator altitude, and your
    signature image.

  What it does not save
    Your flights (that is the logbook backup), your airport
    database, your Work Directory choice, or your Opening
    Balance.

  Where the file goes
    The Work Directory. Filename: logbook_prefs_<date>.json.

  How to use it
    1. On your old phone, open Preferences → Backup / Restore
       Settings → Save Settings.
    2. Copy the logbook_prefs_*.json file from the Work
       Directory to your new phone's Work Directory (or to any
       accessible location).
    3. On your new phone, open Preferences → Backup / Restore
       Settings → Restore Settings.
    4. Confirm the dialog, pick the file, done.

  Signature in the backup
    Your signature image is stored inside the JSON file. On
    restore it is written to the app's internal storage and set
    as your current signature automatically.
